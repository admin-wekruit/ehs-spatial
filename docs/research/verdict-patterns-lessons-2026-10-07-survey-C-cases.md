# How mature systems do "representation → rules → verdict → explanation": a cross-domain survey for Panoptes

Scope: nine domains, each as input → representation → rule language → verdict and uncertainty handling → explanation → validation → failure modes/adoption → lesson. Claims carry URLs; items I could not verify from a primary source are marked **unverified**.

## 1. AEC / BIM code compliance

**Input/representation.** IFC object models. [Eastman et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2009.07.002) split checking into four stages (rule interpretation, model preparation, execution, reporting) and called interpretation the hardest. [buildingSMART IDS](https://www.buildingsmart.org/standards/bsi-standards/information-delivery-specifications-ids/) makes each rule a pair *applicability* (which entities, via six facet types) + *requirements*, so "what the rule applies to" is explicit and machine-checkable before the requirement is evaluated.
**Rules.** [Solibri](https://help.solibri.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500005009042-Understanding-Checking) ships 50+ parametric rule templates; users set parameters, never write logic. Severity (critical/moderate/low) is derived automatically from "how far off a requirement is", humans mark results accepted/rejected, and output is BCF/PDF ([Solibri](https://www.solibri.com/articles/understanding-the-severity-levels-of-checking-results)). EU [ACCORD](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101056973/results/de) marks up clauses with RASE (Requirement/Applicability/Selection/Exception) and compiles them to SHACL shapes over the AEC3PO ontology; one Turtle file is both human- and machine-readable ([CEUR paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3633/paper13.pdf)). [I-SNACC](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10420120) (Wu, Xue & Zhang 2023 — note: not Zhang & El-Gohary) chains NLP → logic rules → reasoning and reports 95.2% precision / 100% recall on IBC 2015 Ch. 10 over two projects; the earlier Zhang & El-Gohary extraction work reported P 0.969 / R 0.944 on IBC 2009 ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10324475)). [Zhang, Teizer et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2012.05.006) encoded OSHA fall-protection rules against BIM + schedule; the follow-up ([Safety Science 2015](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/bim-based-fall-hazard-identification-and-prevention-in-constructi/)) tested on two Finnish buildings.
**Verdict/uncertainty.** Binary issue/no-issue with severity; no formal "cannot determine" state (unverified absence in Solibri).
**Failure modes / adoption.** [Zou et al. 2023](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/abs/10.1061/JMENEA.MEENG-5051) (20 experts, 8 countries) found 12 adoption variables; the four technical ones are system integration, a BIM modelling standard, an information standard, and *a standard way of interpreting the code*. They cite roughly one expert-day to translate and QC one page of code (NZ), regulations "not precise enough" (Estonia), and frequent code updates (UK). Practitioners on Solibri's forum report stale/false results that force re-checking ([Solibri Society](https://society.solibri.com/post/13642)).
**Lesson.** After 15 years the bottleneck is not execution but a QC'd, versioned, clause-aligned rule pack plus a model-preparation standard; Panoptes' "LLM compiles prose, refuses the inexpressible" is exactly variable V12 and must be treated as a product, with IDS-style explicit applicability.

## 2. Construction-site hazard detection from images

**Pipeline.** [Fang et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926580519309082) build a knowledge graph from CV detections + an ontology and infer hazards (falls from height, PPE, collapse, electrical) by rule-based inference; the abstract gives no accuracy figures (**unverified** numbers). [Zhang, Boukamp & Teizer 2015](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/ontology-based-semantic-modeling-of-knowledge-in-construction-cla/) formalised product/process/safety ontologies for JHA — the knowledge-authoring effort is the paper. [Liu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings13020377) run Mask R-CNN + scene-graph relation prediction and match the triples against BERT-extracted regulation triples; accuracies only in the paywalled full text (**unverified**). A 2024 ECAM paper feeds YOLOv5 + panoptic + DeepSORT into a graph database and runs rules over it, again with no accuracy in the abstract ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/ECAM-05-2024-0622/full/html)). A 2025 multimodal-agent framework reports PPE F1 0.84 ([Buildings 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15244439)).
**Failure modes.** (i) Perception errors propagate: every hazard rule is only as good as the edge the detector drew. (ii) OWA/CWA: OWL/SWRL are open-world and monotonic, so "no helmet detected" cannot be concluded as "no helmet" without an explicit closure step ([Hitzler & Parsia](https://www.cs.ksu.edu/~hitzler/resources/publications/chapterHitzlerParsia2008.pdf)). (iii) Rule authoring is manual and evaluation is on self-built datasets; nothing in this line reports a deployment.
**Lesson.** Absence must be a first-class value: a missing edge should yield NEEDS_INPUT/CANNOT_DETERMINE, never a silent PASS or FAIL, and per-class detector recall belongs in the evidence chain.

## 3. Autonomous driving

**RSS.** [Shalev-Shwartz et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.06374v6) define safe longitudinal distance from velocities, response time ρ, and bounded accel/brake values, and the "proper response" when violated. Perception is handled *outside* the rule: a PAC sensing model plus redundant modalities is meant to keep sensing-caused fatalities under 10⁻⁹/h; critics note the sensing assumptions are underspecified ([2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15952)) and propose "imprecise world models" where a verdict must hold for every world model consistent with perception confidence ([Hasuo 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.03418)).
**Formal traffic rules.** [Maierhofer et al. 2020](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1552345/1552345.pdf) encode StVO + Vienna Convention + court decisions in temporal logic and evaluate on recorded data of >2,500 vehicles; [Esterle et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00330) do the same in LTL with a legal-study methodology.
**Perception monitoring.** [PerceMon](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08289) (TQTL/STQL) and [STPL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.14372) put a *specification on the perception output itself* (object persistence, classification consistency, spatial relations). Scene-graph monitors evolved from [SGSM, ICRA 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10596359) to [Scene Flow Specifications, FSE 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3729382) — which found scene graphs too weak for temporal properties and added symbolic entities persisting across time — and [Abstract Scene Graphs, FMAS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14430); an [ICSE 2025 demo](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/icse-2025-demonstrations/12/Closing-the-Gap-between-Sensor-Inputs-and-Driving-Properties-A-Scene-Graph-Generator) exists purely to generate the graph the properties are written over.
**SOTIF.** [ISO 21448:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/77490.html) treats perception insufficiency as a hazard without a fault; scenarios are partitioned known/unknown × safe/unsafe and the goal is to shrink the unknown-unsafe region ([itemis](https://www.itemis.com/en/glossary/sotif/)).
**Lesson.** Write rules over an abstract scene with persistent entity identity, give perception its own monitored spec, and keep an explicit "unknown" class; AD learned that omission is not safety.

## 4. Robotics 3D scene graphs

[Hydra](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13360) builds layered graphs (mesh → places → rooms → buildings) online; [Clio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13696v1) clusters primitives by task relevance and claims improved task-execution accuracy; [ConceptGraphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16650) is open-vocabulary and had to evaluate its graph with AMT raters; [SayPlan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06135) searches a *collapsed* graph and repairs plans with a graph simulator across 3 floors/36 rooms/140 assets. The [MIT 2025 "Structured Interfaces" study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16643) is the cleanest ablation: giving the LLM a Cypher query tool over a graph DB beats serialising the graph into context and beats code generation, scaling to large graphs with far fewer tokens. Note what is measured: task success and scalability, never human interpretability.
**Predicates are the weak link.** On 3DSSG (160 classes, 26 predicates) the CVPR 2020 model reaches predicate R@3 0.89 but full triplet R@50 only 0.40 ([Wald et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03967)). In VQA, ground-truth scene graphs give 94.6% on GQA vs ~52.9% with generated graphs, whose edges overlapped the truth in only 1–2 places ([Understanding the Role of Scene Graphs in VQA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.05479)).
**Lesson.** The graph earns its keep as a queryable, provenance-carrying *interface*, not as "understanding"; learned categorical predicates fail, metric relations computed from geometry (Panoptes' typed distances/gaps/occupancy) are the kind that survive.

## 5. Neuro-symbolic visual reasoning

[NS-VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02338v1) parses scene + program and executes symbolically (99.8% CLEVR), with each step inspectable. [VisProg](https://prior.allenai.org/projects/visprog) and [ViperGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08128) have an LLM write a program over vision APIs with no training; the stated benefit is a rationale. Follow-up analysis uses that rationale to attribute failures to individual modules ([2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01974)); secondary sources attribute most VisProg errors to the vision modules rather than the program (**unverified** against the primary). [Logic-LM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.12295) reports the number Panoptes should copy: executable-formulation rate per dataset (GPT-4: 99–100% on synthetic sets, 79.9% FOLIO, 32.6% AR-LSAT); unparseable cases fall back to chain-of-thought, and feeding solver errors back raises executability ~5 points.
**Lesson.** Publish the spec→rule compile rate as a KPI, keep the non-compilable bucket explicit (never a soft fallback), and expect symbol/perception errors, not reasoning errors, to dominate.

## 6. Rules as code / legal engineering

[Catala](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03198) makes default logic first-class (base case and exceptions written where the statute writes them), aligns code paragraph-by-paragraph with the text, proves its compiler in F*, and found a bug in an official French benefits implementation; [CUTECat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18212) adds concolic test generation covering all branches. [OpenFisca](https://openfisca.readthedocs.io/en/latest/coding-the-legislation/40_legislation_evolutions.html) stores every parameter as dated values with a `reference` to the legal source. Government lessons: NZ [Better Rules](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/95/html) found multidisciplinary teams "forced conceptual consistency" and surfaced hidden assumptions; Canada reported that interpreting meaning was the hard part and that imperative code cannot capture the whole law ([OECD OPSI 2024](https://oecd-opsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Rules-as-Code-in-Canada.pdf)); OECD's [Cracking the Code](https://oecd-opsi.org/publications/cracking-the-code/) requires traceability, accountability and *appealability*. [LegalRuleML](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=7617) standardises defeasibility, temporal validity, and isomorphism between rule and provision.
**Lesson.** A rule pack is a legal artefact: clause-aligned, defeasible, dated, test-covered, signed off by a domain expert, and contestable — the verdict must carry what would overturn it.

## 7. Clinical decision support

Arden Syntax MLMs are event-condition-action rules; the "curly braces problem" (site-specific data bindings) is why MLMs never shared well ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2233583)). Missing data is handled by *three-valued logic*: Arden has a null type and TRUE/FALSE/NULL operators ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arden_syntax)); CQL defines 3VL "for reasoning in the presence of missing information" ([CQL spec](https://cql.hl7.org/13-f-glossary.html)); CDS Hooks forbids null placeholders ([HL7](https://cds-hooks.hl7.org/2.0)). [Peleg et al. 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC150359) compared six guideline models and found no consensus on core components. Formalising guidelines reliably exposes spec defects: decision-table analysis found incompleteness and inconsistency ([Shiffman & Greenes 1994](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/medema/v14y1994i3p245-254.html)); anomaly detection on Asbru found ambiguity and omissions ([Duftschmid & Miksch 2001](https://www.cvast.tuwien.ac.at/sites/default/files/bibcite/96/duf_aimj2001.pdf)). Deployment reality: override rates 46–96%, driven by low specificity ([Poly et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7400042/)); Bates' [ten commandments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC264429) start with speed and fitting the workflow. The knowledge-acquisition bottleneck dates to [Feigenbaum 1977](https://people.dbmi.columbia.edu/shortliffe/Buchanan-Shortliffe-1984/Chapter-07.pdf) and is still the top issue in a [2017 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5517823/).
**Lesson.** Propagate "unknown" through every operator and emit an explicit insufficient-data verdict; false positives, not missed hazards, are what make users stop reading; route every ambiguity the compiler finds back to the spec owner.

## 8. Industrial / functional safety practice

[ISO 12100](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) makes risk assessment a documented, human-owned process; [ISO 13849-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html) quantifies control reliability. [SISTEMA](https://dguv.de/ifa/praxishilfen/practical-solutions-machine-safety/software-sistema/index.jsp) computes PFHd/PL from category, MTTFd, DCavg and CCF; the required PLr is typed in by the engineer, and the tool is explicit that the simplified method only applies to designated architectures — otherwise Markov modelling is needed ([SISTEMA Cookbook 4](https://dguv.de/medien/ifa/en/pra/softwa/sistema/kochbuch/sistema_cookbook4_v2_en.pdf)). Pilz [PAScal](https://www.controleng.com/?p=90998) is the same contract. [SICK Safety Designer](https://www.sick.com/in/en/c/g575306) draws protective fields on *measured* contours and emits configuration documentation; [ISO 13855:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/80590.html) gives S = K·T + C with K, T, C as declared inputs; [ISO 13857:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/69569.html) gives reach tables. Automated verification from 3D data is thin: [Seim et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-10071-0_9) generate a laser-measurement program from a digital twin but a safety engineer still picks the points; an online distance tracker exists only in simulation with static scenes ([PMC 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10057077/)); a patent covers verifying safety volumes of a movable unit ([US 10,888,998](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10888998), assignee **unverified**). I found no deployed photo-based safeguard verifier (**unverified** absence).
**Lesson.** Every safety tool computes a narrow quantity and refuses outside its model; stopping time, approach speed and PLr are declared, not inferred — Panoptes should demand them as NEEDS_INPUT rather than guess.

## 9. Metrology conformity decisions

ISO/IEC 17025:2017 requires the decision rule to be stated; [ILAC-G8:09/2019](https://ilac.org/?p=122723) defines tolerance limit, acceptance limit and guard band *w*, and the non-binary statement set: pass (false-accept ≈2.5% at w = U), conditional pass (inside the guard band, risk up to 50%), conditional fail, fail ([summary](https://www.itc.gov.hk/en/quality/scl/doc/scl/talk_1.pdf)). [ISO 14253-1:2017](https://www.evs.ee/en/iso-14253-1-2017) gives default rules when results fall in the uncertainty zone; [JCGM 106:2012](https://www.oiml.org/en/files/pdf_g/g001-106-e12.pdf) separates specific risk (this item) from global risk and computes conformance probability. [UKAS LAB 48](https://ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/LAB-48-Decision-rules-and-statements-of-conformity.pdf) gives report wording.
**Lesson.** Adopt the four-state statement verbatim, print the decision rule and U on every verdict, and treat "conditional" as a distinct state, not a rounding choice.

## Synthesis

### (a) Recurring patterns

| Pattern | Who uses it | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed vocabulary between perception and rules | IDS facets; AEC3PO/SHACL; STPL/scene-graph monitors; CQL value sets | Rules are checkable for expressibility; perception can be spec'd and tested separately | Every new predicate is a schema change; open-vocabulary detectors must be mapped |
| Explicit unknown/insufficient-data state | Arden/CQL 3VL; ILAC conditional pass/fail; SOTIF unknown-unsafe; SISTEMA "not applicable" | Stops silent PASS by omission; makes gaps actionable | More verdict classes to explain; users must accept "we don't know" |
| Rules traceable to clauses, with tests | Catala, LegalRuleML isomorphism, RASE, OpenFisca references, CUTECat | Auditability; sign-off; cheap re-verification when the standard changes | Clause-level authoring effort (~1 expert-day/page, Zou 2023) |
| Symbolic executor + ML perception, never ML as judge | NS-VQA, VisProg/ViperGPT, Logic-LM, I-SNACC, SGSM | Deterministic, inspectable verdicts; failures attributable to a module | Compile-rate ceiling (Logic-LM 33–80% on real text); brittle to symbol errors |
| Explanation as evidence chain | Solibri issues + BCF; CDS alert rationale; NS-VQA traces; Safety Designer documentation | Users can contest a verdict; false positives get triaged | Must store per-step provenance, not just results |
| Versioned rule packs | OpenFisca dated parameters; LegalRuleML temporal; SISTEMA 1.x/2.x/3.x per ISO edition | Verdicts reproducible against a named standard edition | Migration and regression testing per edition |
| Uncertainty-aware thresholds | ILAC-G8 guard bands; JCGM 106; RSS parameter bounds | Honest pass/fail near limits; stated risk | Need credible U per measurement, or the guard band is fiction |
| Human applicability sign-off | IDS applicability; ISO 12100 risk assessment; PLr typed into SISTEMA; RaC appealability | Scope errors caught before rules run | Slows fully automatic use; needs a UI for "this rule applies here because…" |

### (b) Recurring failure modes
- **Knowledge-acquisition bottleneck**: from Feigenbaum to Zou 2023 the cost is interpreting the spec, not running it.
- **Perception errors propagating**: GQA's 94.6→52.9 drop and the 3DSSG triplet recall of 0.40 are the shape of the risk; construction-safety papers rarely report end-to-end precision.
- **OWA/CWA confusion**: OWL/SWRL cannot infer "missing PPE"; AD monitors had to add explicit entity persistence.
- **Rule drift**: codes change (UK), guideline editions change, ISO 13849-1 has three SISTEMA generations.
- **Over-formalisation without users**: six CIG models without consensus; decades of ACC prototypes with low adoption.
- **Interpretability claims without measurement**: VisProg/ViperGPT and scene-graph planners assert interpretability but measure task success; alert-fatigue data shows what unmeasured explanations do in practice.

### (c) Does a scene graph help understanding?
The evidence says the graph helps as an *interface and evidence substrate*, not as understanding. Where graphs demonstrably improved outcomes, the gain came from retrieval and scale (SayPlan's collapsed-graph search; MIT 2025's Cypher interface beating context serialisation) or from writing specifications over a stable semantic space instead of pixels (SGSM → Scene Flow → ASG). Where the graph was asked to *supply* semantics, it underperformed: predicted predicates are the weakest numbers in 3DSSG and the reason generated scene graphs lose ~40 points on GQA. For Panoptes this is good news: its edges are metric quantities computed from reconstruction (distance, gap, overlap, line of sight, occupancy), not learned predicates, so the graph is a typed, queryable ledger of measurements with uncertainty attached — exactly the role the AD and robotics results support. Keep it thin; do not add learned relation classes; expose it as a query interface (Spark-DSG export is consistent with the MIT finding).

### (d) Prioritised lessons for Panoptes
1. **Report metrology-style verdicts** (case 9): value ± U, decision rule, guard band, four-state statement; NEEDS_MEASUREMENT is your "conditional".
2. **Make absence a value** (cases 2, 7): three-valued propagation through every relation; a missing object or edge yields NEEDS_INPUT/CANNOT_DETERMINE, never PASS.
3. **Treat the rule pack as the product** (cases 1, 6): clause-aligned, RASE-style applicability/exception markup, dated against the standard edition, with tests; budget ~1 expert-day per spec page for QC.
4. **Measure and publish the compile rate** (case 5): fraction of spec clauses the LLM compiles, refuses, and has to be hand-fixed; the refusal bucket is a feature.
5. **Declared inputs stay declared** (case 8): stopping time, approach speed, PLr, reference-object dimension are NEEDS_INPUT; never infer them from photos.
6. **Spec the perception layer separately** (case 3): STPL-style checks on reconstruction output (object persistence across views, dimension consistency, floor contact) before rules run.
7. **Explicit applicability sign-off** (cases 1, 8): a human confirms which rules apply to this cell before verdicts, IDS/ISO 12100 style.
8. **Evidence chain per verdict** (cases 1, 7): the photo crop, the mesh/box, the measured value, U, the clause, the rule version — enough to contest it (RaC appealability).
9. **Optimise precision over recall of FAIL** (case 7): override rates show that low-specificity alerts kill adoption; prefer CANNOT_DETERMINE to speculative FAIL.
10. **Keep the graph thin and queryable** (case 4): metric relations only; expose a query API; do not let the graph become the "understanding".
11. **Track unknown-unsafe** (case 3): log cases outside the rule vocabulary as a SOTIF-style backlog rather than silently out of scope.
12. **Expect formalisation to find spec bugs** (cases 6, 7): Catala and guideline verification both found errors in official texts; route them to the spec author, not into the rule.
